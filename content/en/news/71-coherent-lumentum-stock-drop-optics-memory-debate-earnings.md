## What Happened

On Monday, August 10, shares of the AI data-center optical component makers that have anchored one of this year's hottest trades fell sharply in tandem. Coherent (NYSE: COHR), the sector's bellwether, slid from a prior close of $379.13 to around $335, a drop of roughly 11-12% intraday. Rival Lumentum Holdings (Nasdaq: LITE) fell to around $830, down roughly 7-9% on the day. Both stocks had been treated as prime beneficiaries of the AI infrastructure buildout, so a same-day double-digit-percentage decline in both names was a notable break from the recent pattern.

What made the move especially noteworthy was the absence of any single, obvious trigger. There was no earnings warning, no company disclosure, and no confirmed analyst downgrade behind the drop. Instead, market commentary pointed to an "optics vs. memory" debate that spread across social platform X over the weekend. Citrini Research analyst Jukan had posted a trading thesis suggesting that, in the near term, investors consider "selling memory, buying optics" - citing pressures including forced redemptions in leveraged memory-chip ETFs as one factor behind the idea. Ironically, a debate framed as bullish for the optical sector appears to have reminded the market just how far optical stocks had already run, triggering profit-taking rather than fresh buying.

To understand the scale of the pullback, the run-up that preceded it matters just as much. Coherent had surged 44.22% in the single week leading into this decline and was up 105.41% year to date before Monday's session. Lumentum had climbed an even steeper 142% year to date. Framed that way, Monday's move reads less like a fundamental sell-off and more like a pause for breath after an unusually fast climb, timed just ahead of both companies' earnings reports. The valuation numbers make the case even more directly: Coherent was trading at a trailing twelve-month P/E of roughly 160x, and Lumentum at roughly 146x - multiples that reflect a market already pricing in something close to flawless execution. With that much optimism baked in and no fresh positive catalyst arriving, pre-earnings de-risking became a plausible - and, judging by the price action, popular - trade.

The timing adds another layer. Lumentum is scheduled to report fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 results after the close on Tuesday, August 11, with Coherent following a day later, after the close on Wednesday, August 12. Much of the sell-side commentary around Monday's decline described it as a textbook pre-earnings de-risking move rather than a reaction to deteriorating fundamentals.

## Why Two Stocks Fell in Tandem Without a Clear Catalyst

The most useful way to read this episode is as a story about positioning and valuation rather than company-specific weakness. The first driver is the outsized influence of leveraged ETFs and short-term trading flows. Leveraged products tied to AI themes are built to amplify the underlying assets' moves several times over, which means that even a modest shift in sentiment can trigger large forced rebalancing trades. As the "sell memory, buy optics" idea circulated, leveraged memory-linked products likely saw outflows at the same time profit-taking hit optical names that had already run hot - two flows that could easily have reinforced each other on the same trading day.

The second driver is the structural fragility of stocks already priced for perfection. P/E ratios north of 140x mean the market has effectively pulled several years of expected growth forward into today's share price. At that kind of valuation, even earnings that beat consensus can trigger a sell-off, because the bar the market has set is already so elevated that "very good" results can still disappoint relative to what's implied by the price. Experienced investors often respond to this setup by trimming exposure ahead of the print rather than waiting to see how the report lands - precisely the behavior Monday's action suggests played out here.

The third driver is rotation within the broader AI infrastructure value chain. Throughout this year, capital has moved quickly between sub-sectors of the AI trade - GPUs, memory chips, power infrastructure, data centers, and optical networking components - as each segment takes its turn being the market's preferred way to express the AI investment theme. When one segment runs too far too fast, money often rotates into whichever segment has been relatively left behind, and once that new leader gets overheated, the rotation continues elsewhere. This week's optical-sector pullback fits that pattern, and it's a reminder that volatility within specific corners of the AI trade is likely to persist as capital keeps moving between them.

## What to Take Away From This

- **Stocks can move sharply even without a clear negative headline.** This decline wasn't driven by weak earnings or bad company news - it was driven by a social-media trading idea colliding with profit-taking instincts. The absence of an obvious "reason" doesn't make a sell-off irrational; crowded positioning can itself be the risk factor.
- **Stocks trading well above 100x trailing earnings should be expected to see amplified volatility around earnings dates.** At that kind of valuation, the market's bar is already set high enough that even a modest beat can read as a disappointment. As with [Datadog's 19% plunge despite 36% revenue growth over a guidance-language concern](/en/news/datadog-stock-rebound-openai-usage-cut-analyst-price-targets/), investors in richly valued growth names should factor in wider-than-usual price swings around results.
- **A stock that's up 40%+ in a single week is a reasonable candidate for pre-earnings profit-taking.** Selling into a rapid rally ahead of a known catalyst - an earnings date - is a common institutional behavior, not necessarily a signal that the underlying growth story has changed. A pullback like this one is worth reading as a pause tied to a specific upcoming event rather than an automatic trend reversal.
- **Themes with heavy leveraged-ETF participation can see outsized swings on relatively small pieces of news.** When a large share of the money in a theme sits in leveraged vehicles, price action can decouple from fundamentals for a session or more, simply because of how those products rebalance.
- **Understanding rotation within a broader theme like AI infrastructure helps put single-stock drops in context.** When one part of the AI chain gets a boost, other sub-sectors can simultaneously see capital rotate out - watching the whole value chain, not just one stock, gives a fuller picture of what's actually driving any single move.

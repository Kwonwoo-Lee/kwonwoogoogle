## "Have You Heard of KONEX?" — Just One New Listing This Year

In mid-2026, a Korean business outlet ran a story asking, roughly, "Have you heard of KONEX?" — noting that only one new company had listed on the exchange all year. KONEX is an official exchange market Korea Exchange launched in 2013 for venture and small businesses not yet ready for Kosdaq. It sits right alongside Kospi and Kosdaq in Korea's exchange hierarchy, yet most investors have little idea what's happening to it: traded names fell to 108 by late July 2026, down from around 150 in early 2020, with one report citing average daily trading value near its lowest level since 2014. This lesson covers how KONEX actually works, how it differs from Kosdaq and K-OTC, and why it's been quietly emptying out — not whether any specific KONEX stock is worth buying, which belongs to the strategies course.

## An Official Exchange, One Tier Below Kosdaq

KONEX (Korea New Exchange) is a fully official listed market run by Korea Exchange, just like Kospi and Kosdaq. Unlike [K-OTC](/en/basics/k-otc-market-explained/), which trades shares over the counter outside the exchange system, KONEX stocks go through a real listing process — the bar is simply set much lower. Reports put its equity, revenue, and market-cap thresholds at roughly one-third to one-tenth of Kosdaq's, making room for early-stage ventures with real technology but not yet Kosdaq-scale revenue. KONEX was never meant to be a destination in itself — it's a formal on-ramp toward a bigger market.

## The Designated Advisor: A Brokerage Does the Exchange's Job

What structurally sets KONEX apart is its **designated advisor** system. A Kosdaq listing requires a direct preliminary review by the exchange itself. A KONEX company instead appoints a brokerage as its designated advisor, which performs that first-line screening and, after listing, keeps two ongoing jobs: providing liquidity by continuously quoting both sides of thin stocks, and acting as a disclosure proxy for companies without in-house reporting infrastructure. This keeps the bar low without forcing deep exchange reviews on every tiny firm — but the fee falls on the company, and if trading stays too thin for the brokerage to earn much, it has less reason to stay on. If a contract lapses and no replacement is found in time, the company can be delisted outright: the advisor relationship is literally load-bearing.

## Why the 30-Million-Won Deposit Disappeared

KONEX originally required investors to deposit 30 million won with a brokerage before trading, or use a special small-investor account capped at 30 million won a year — meant to keep out investors who couldn't absorb the risk of younger, less transparent companies. But the protection choked the market it protected: a narrower investor pool meant thinner trading, which meant a heavier liquidity burden on designated advisors. In 2022, regulators scrapped both rules entirely. Since then, anyone can trade KONEX stocks exactly like any Kospi or Kosdaq name — trading away protection friction for a shot at reviving liquidity.

## Moving to Kosdaq: Regular vs. Fast-Track Transfer

A KONEX company with a track record can move to Kosdaq through a [transfer listing](/en/basics/transfer-listing-kosdaq-to-kospi/). A regular transfer is reviewed like a fresh Kosdaq IPO; a fast-track transfer eases that bar for companies with a solid KONEX history — a 2022 reform cut its revenue-growth threshold from 20% to 10% and added a track based purely on market cap and liquidity. This has been KONEX's main selling point: roughly 8 companies a year have transferred since 2013, crossing 100 cumulative by mid-2025. But the pipeline is drying up — transfers peaked at 13 in 2021, fell to single digits in 2022–2023, and one 2026 report counted zero.

## A Structural Problem, Not a Cyclical One

New KONEX listings averaged around 27 a year as recently as 2023, then fell to 6 in 2024, 4 in 2025, and just one or two in 2026. The main reason: faster paths onto Kosdaq itself have widened, with special listing tracks for tech and high-growth firms letting unprofitable companies skip KONEX entirely. The advisor-economics problem compounds it — fewer actively traded names mean fewer brokerages willing to take on the role, discouraging new listings further.

## The Risk Investors Should Weigh

The first risk is liquidity: a meaningful share of KONEX names go entire days with zero volume. The second is information asymmetry, since disclosure depends heavily on the advisor rather than in-house reporting. The third is advisor attrition — a company can be delisted simply because its advisor walks away, regardless of the underlying business.

## A Numeric Illustration

Imagine hypothetical venture firm M Corp: too small for Kosdaq, comfortably within KONEX's bar. It pays a brokerage several tens of millions of won a year as its advisor. If shares trade actively, commissions partly offset that fee; if the stock goes days without a trade, the advisor may decline to renew, and M Corp risks delisting without a replacement. Grow enough to clear the fast-track threshold instead, and the same company opens a path into Kosdaq's much larger investor base — the tightrope every KONEX company walks.

## Key Takeaways

- KONEX is an official Korea Exchange market with a much lower bar than Kosdaq, meant for venture and small businesses.
- A brokerage, not the exchange, screens eligibility and provides liquidity as designated advisor — and a failed advisor relationship can mean delisting.
- The 30-million-won deposit and special small-investor account were scrapped in 2022 to revive liquidity.
- Regular and fast-track transfer listings lead to Kosdaq, but far fewer companies are using that path now.
- Wider direct-to-Kosdaq listing tracks have eroded KONEX's "stepping stone" role, driving a structural decline in both listings and trading.

## FAQ

### Is KONEX the same as K-OTC?
No. KONEX is an official listed market with a formal listing process; K-OTC lets shares of never-listed companies trade over the counter under a different operator entirely.

### Do I still need a deposit to invest in KONEX?
No. The 30-million-won deposit and special account were both scrapped in 2022. KONEX now trades like any Kospi or Kosdaq stock.

### What happens if a KONEX company never reaches Kosdaq?
It isn't automatically delisted, but it can stay on KONEX indefinitely — and separately risks delisting if it loses its advisor or trading collapses.

## What Happened

Kospi opened sharply lower on August 6, falling 119.51 points, or 1.81%, to 6,478.75. The selling accelerated through the morning, and the index briefly plunged as much as 5% before the Korea Exchange (KRX) stepped in. At 10:18:12 a.m. local time, the exchange triggered a sell-side sidecar - a circuit breaker that automatically activates when Kospi 200 futures fall 5% or more below their reference price and stay there for at least a minute, suspending program-trading orders for five minutes to give the market a brief cooling-off period.

The two chip bellwethers that drive much of the index bore the brunt of the selling. As of 9:34 a.m., **Samsung Electronics** was down 3.66% at 237,000 won, while **SK Hynix** had cratered 7.13% to 1,549,000 won. Foreign investors dumped more than 500 billion won worth of Kospi shares in net selling that morning, while retail investors did the opposite - reversing an early sell-off of their own to become net buyers and absorb much of that supply.

The timing makes this especially notable: barely 24 hours earlier, on August 5, Kospi had jumped 3.85% to reclaim the 6,600 level on optimism around a Strait of Hormuz deal, with Samsung and SK Hynix both rallying hard. Less than a full trading day later, the exact same two stocks were driving the index in the opposite direction.

## Why the Rally Flipped Into a Crash So Quickly

The root of this reversal traces back across the Pacific to guidance from US memory chipmakers.

- **A SanDisk guidance shock started the chain reaction.** NAND flash specialist SanDisk issued revenue guidance that fell short of Wall Street's expectations, and shares sold off sharply. Western Digital and Micron were dragged down in the same session. All three had ridden the AI-driven memory "supercycle" narrative and data-center demand to near-record highs in recent weeks, which meant a single disappointing guidance number was enough to shake the market's entire framework for pricing the sector.
- **Valuations had gotten stretched.** SK Hynix's own most recent earnings report showed explosive year-over-year growth in both revenue and profit, yet still landed slightly below consensus - enough to reignite debate over whether expectations for AI memory demand had simply run ahead of reality. Bulls would note the underlying business is still strong, but after months of steep gains, even a minor disappointment was enough to trigger profit-taking.
- **Nasdaq weakness overnight set the tone before Asia even opened.** On August 5 in New York, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.83%, snapping a five-day winning streak, even as the Dow closed at a fresh record. SpaceX and AMD both fell after their earnings releases on concerns about the scale of AI infrastructure spending, and that risk-off undertone in US tech was already weighing on sentiment heading into the Seoul and Tokyo sessions.
- **Kospi's market-cap weighting amplified the move in both directions.** The same structural feature that powered the prior day's rally - Samsung and SK Hynix's outsized combined weight in the index - worked in reverse this time. With two stocks capable of swinging the entire index, SK Hynix's 7%-plus drop alone did much of the work in dragging Kospi down to sidecar-triggering territory.

Notably, some major investment banks stuck with their bullish calls through the selloff. Analysts at Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan reportedly maintained buy ratings on both stocks, pointing out that forward price-to-earnings ratios near 3.5x-3.6x looked detached from the companies' underlying fundamentals - framing the drop as sentiment-driven rather than a reassessment of the AI memory demand story itself.

## What a Sell-Side Sidecar Actually Does

The Korea Exchange's sidecar mechanism exists to stop program trading - automated, algorithm-driven order flow - from mechanically amplifying a sharp price move. It activates automatically once Kospi 200 futures fall (or rise) 5% or more from their reference price and hold that level for a full minute, at which point program-trading orders are suspended for five minutes. The goal isn't to prevent a decline outright; it's to interrupt the feedback loop where algorithms react to each other's sell signals in a self-reinforcing spiral, and to buy human investors a few minutes to reassess calmly rather than get swept up in the momentum. The fact that retail investors flipped to net buyers shortly after the sidecar activated is a reasonable real-world example of that cooling-off period working as intended.

## What to Take Away From This

- **Rallies and crashes can be born from the same structural feature.** The outsized index weight of Samsung and SK Hynix drove Kospi's rally one day and amplified its crash the next - for exactly the same mechanical reason. If you hold a market-cap-weighted index that's concentrated in a couple of mega-caps, you're accepting both the upside torque and the downside volatility that comes with that concentration.
- **Guidance is judged against expectations already priced in, not against some absolute bar.** SanDisk's guidance may not have been objectively bad - the more relevant question is how far the stock had run in the weeks before the report, and how much optimism the market had already baked into the price. Reading an earnings reaction well means checking the stock's recent run-up and its position relative to consensus, not just the headline number.
- **A circuit breaker triggering is evidence the system is working, not evidence of panic.** Headlines about a sidecar or circuit breaker activating can read as alarming, but understanding what the mechanism actually does - pausing algorithmic order flow for five minutes - makes it easier to separate a structural safety valve from a genuine fundamental breakdown.
- **Global supply chains can flip sentiment within half a trading day.** A single guidance disclosure from a US memory maker crossed the Pacific and moved the entire Korean stock market before Seoul's morning coffee had cooled. Anyone holding Korean semiconductor names should be tracking the US memory trio's (Micron, Western Digital, SanDisk) earnings calendars just as closely as domestic news.

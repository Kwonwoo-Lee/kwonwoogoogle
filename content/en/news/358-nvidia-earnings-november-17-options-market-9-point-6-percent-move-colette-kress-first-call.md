## What Happened

Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) reports fiscal third-quarter 2027 earnings (the period ended October 25, 2026) after the market closes on Tuesday, November 17. The release is expected around 1:20 p.m. Pacific time, with the conference call following at 2 p.m. Pacific (5 p.m. Eastern). What makes this particular earnings call different from the dozen or so that preceded it isn't the numbers themselves - it's who delivers them. This is the first full investor call since Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO, and board member, effective immediately, on November 12. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress has stepped in as interim CEO while keeping her CFO title, and Tuesday's call will be her first time addressing the entire investor base in that dual capacity.

How seriously the market is taking this particular report shows up directly in options pricing. As of Thursday, November 12's close, options traders were pricing in roughly a 9.6% move for Nvidia shares following the report - the largest expected swing since 2024. That's a notable number for a stock that has posted close-to-10% earnings-day moves fairly routinely this year, which tells you something extra is riding on this one beyond the usual quarterly suspense: a second, entirely different layer of uncertainty tied to who's actually running the company.

Start with the hard numbers. Back on August 26, when Nvidia reported second-quarter results, it guided third-quarter revenue to $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, with non-GAAP gross margin of 74.0%, plus or minus 50 basis points. That margin figure is a full percentage point below the second quarter's 75.0%, which management attributed to rising memory costs as the newest systems ramp to volume production. Critically, that guidance assumes zero data center compute revenue from China - meaning any reopening of that market, even partial, would represent pure upside against the guide rather than something baked in.

Analyst consensus heading into the report sits above that guidance. Figures vary slightly by data provider, but LSEG-based consensus points to roughly $109.2 billion in revenue and adjusted earnings per share around $2.47 to $2.48, while Zacks puts the numbers at about $108.99 billion and $2.49. A consensus revenue figure near the top of Nvidia's own guided range (which tops out near $110.2 billion at the high end of the plus-2% band) tells you the market is pricing in Nvidia doing what it has done almost every quarter for several years running: beating its own guidance. Whether that streak continues is the first thing this report will settle.

For scale, second-quarter revenue came in at a record $96.2 billion, with GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins both at 75.0% and operating income up 124% year over year to $63.7 billion. Against that backdrop, nobody is seriously worried about an outright revenue collapse. The real focus is on the direction of margins. Kress has already flagged that fourth-quarter (fiscal 2027) gross margin could dip further to the 71%-72% range before recovering to roughly 72%-73% in fiscal 2028 - a signal that rising memory costs will keep pressuring profitability for a while yet, tied to the same supply crunch that has been driving volatility in memory names like SanDisk, Micron, and Western Digital in recent months.

## Why This Report Carries Extra Weight

A typical Nvidia earnings call draws investor attention almost entirely to data center revenue growth, the GPU supply-demand balance, and next-quarter guidance. This time, there's an entirely separate layer of questions stacked on top: whether the month-long leadership vacuum and accompanying scandal coverage have left any mark on customer relationships, supply agreements, or the retention of key executives. That's not something a revenue or margin line item answers directly - it's something investors will have to read in management's tone, how questions get framed, and which topics analysts keep circling back to in the Q&A.

To understand why options are pricing in 9.6%, it helps to understand implied volatility. Options prices embed the market's collective estimate of how much a stock is likely to move before a given date. As a known catalyst like an earnings report approaches, implied volatility tends to climb steadily right up until that uncertainty resolves - and once the numbers hit the tape and the market picks a direction, implied volatility typically collapses almost immediately, regardless of which way the stock actually moved. Traders call this volatility crush. The reason Nvidia's current reading is unusually elevated is that the normal earnings-day uncertainty is now stacked on top of a second, structurally different kind of uncertainty: who is actually in charge.

There's also a setup dynamic worth noting. This report lands while Nvidia shares are already under real pressure. The stock closed at $162.85 on Friday, November 13, after HSBC cut its price target from $375 to $170 and downgraded the stock to Hold - a close that pushed Nvidia's market capitalization below the $4 trillion mark and extended the stock's losing streak to six straight weeks. When a stock has already been pushed well below where it traded before a cycle of bad headlines began, market reactions to earnings often hinge less on the absolute numbers and more on whether those numbers clear an already-depressed bar. A modest beat against consensus, paired with any sign that the leadership transition is stabilizing, could do more to repair sentiment than the same beat would in a calmer environment. Conversely, a miss or a further guidance cut would land on top of an already fragile tape rather than a neutral one.

## What to Take Away From This

- **A high implied-volatility reading ahead of earnings tells you about magnitude, not direction.** A 9.6% expected move doesn't say whether Nvidia shares will rise or fall - it says the market expects a large move either way. Buying options purely to bet on an earnings reaction is a common way retail traders lose money even when they correctly guess the direction, because volatility crush after the announcement can erode the option's value faster than the stock's move adds to it.
- **Check the gap between a company's own guidance and the analyst consensus before the print.** When consensus sits near the top of a company's guided range, as it does here, merely "meeting guidance" may not be enough to move the stock higher. What matters is how far the actual result clears the expectations that are already baked into the price, not the headline comparison to the guide itself.
- **A margin trend can carry more signal than a revenue beat.** Kress flagging a further step-down to 71%-72% gross margin for the next quarter, well before this report, tells you the memory-cost pressure isn't a one-quarter blip - it's a structural input analysts should be modeling forward, not treating as a surprise when it eventually shows up.
- **In a leadership-crisis quarter, qualitative signals matter as much as the financial statement.** Commentary on customer contract renewals, executive retention, and any timeline toward naming a permanent CEO can move the stock more than the historical numbers themselves. Reading the call transcript, not just the headline earnings release, is especially worthwhile this time around.

## FAQ

### When exactly does Nvidia report, and where can I watch?
Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter 2027 results after market close on Tuesday, November 17, with the earnings release expected around 1:20 p.m. Pacific time and the conference call at 2 p.m. Pacific (5 p.m. Eastern). The release and a live webcast of the call are available on Nvidia's investor relations website, and most major financial outlets will carry the numbers in real time as they cross.

### Does the 9.6% implied options move mean the stock will go up or down?
Neither. That figure describes the expected magnitude of the move, not its direction. It means options traders collectively expect the stock to swing by roughly that much, up or down, in reaction to the report and call - the actual direction will depend on what the numbers show and what management says on the call.

### Will Colette Kress address a timeline for naming a permanent CEO on this call?
Nothing has been confirmed publicly, but given this is the first full investor call just days into the interim-CEO period, analysts are likely to raise the question directly. Formal CEO succession decisions are typically communicated through separate board disclosures rather than on an earnings call, so the absence of a concrete timeline on Tuesday wouldn't be unusual.

### Is a 74% gross margin guidance actually a problem?
On its own, 74% remains an exceptionally high margin by semiconductor industry standards. The reason it's drawing attention is the trend: down from 75% in the second quarter, with management already signaling a further dip to 71%-72% in the fourth quarter. The trajectory, tied to rising memory costs across the industry, is the more important thing to watch than the absolute level, and it's likely to persist until the broader memory supply crunch eases.

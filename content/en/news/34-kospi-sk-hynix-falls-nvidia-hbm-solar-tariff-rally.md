## What Happened

Kospi opened sharply higher on August 7, gaining 68.69 points (1.09%) to 6,365.07, and extended that rally through the early morning to as much as 1.83% - hitting 6,411.69 by 9:08 a.m. local time and touching an intraday high of 6,415.60. On the surface, it looked like a one-day reversal of the previous session's crash, when a sell-side sidecar had been triggered. Foreign buying drove the early gains, lifting Samsung Electronics 3.15% and SK Hynix 2.54%.

The picture flipped entirely in the afternoon. By 1 p.m., Kospi had fallen 90.73 points (1.44%) to 6,250.65, and the index touched an intraday low of 6,158.73 - a swing of more than 4% from the morning's peak. Kospi ultimately closed down 37.61 points (0.60%) at 6,258.77. The Kosdaq closed at 798.81, down 0.36%, snapping a six-session winning streak. By investor type, foreigners net sold 856.2 billion won, driving the reversal, while individual investors net bought 265.3 billion won and institutions net bought 579 billion won - the same foreign investors who had powered the morning rally flipped to sellers by midday.

The epicenter of the reversal was SK Hynix, which closed down 4.88% at 1,422,000 won. Reports spreading through the session said Nvidia was reviewing lowering the high-bandwidth-memory (HBM) specification for its next-generation "Rubin Ultra" AI accelerator, and the stock's morning gains evaporated as the news circulated. Samsung Electronics, by contrast, closed up 0.22% - a sharp divergence between the two chip bellwethers that normally move together.

A separate story pushed a different sector in the opposite direction entirely. President Trump signed a proclamation on August 6 (local time) setting a minimum import price on polysilicon and polysilicon-derived products, with a 15% ad valorem tariff on some derivatives - a move aimed squarely at Chinese-linked solar material supply chains. Hanwha Solutions, which has US-based production, surged as much as 20.56% to 36,650 won by 9:10 a.m., and OCI Holdings, which sources polysilicon outside China, jumped 14.14% to 278,500 won. By 1 p.m., sector-level losses in metals (-3.03%), machinery/equipment (-2.51%), and electronics (-1.65%) dragged the index down, even as solar-linked materials names moved sharply the other way.

## Why the Same HBM Rumor Hit SK Hynix but Not Samsung

To understand why SK Hynix took the brunt of this news, it helps to look at the story that's been supporting memory chip valuations for months. In June, SK Hynix said it had delivered 12-Hi HBM4E samples to major customers ahead of schedule, cementing its position as the clear frontrunner in next-generation HBM. That "first-mover premium" has been a core pillar of the bullish case behind SK Hynix's stock.

The report circulating Friday attacked exactly that premise. According to the reporting, Nvidia is weighing a mainstream Rubin Ultra configuration using 8-Hi, 192GB HBM4 rather than the previously expected 12-Hi, 288GB HBM4E - a move reportedly driven by tight HBM supply, data-center power constraints, and cost pressure. That's not simply a story about softer demand; it's a story about the very high-spec, high-value product line where SK Hynix claimed the clearest lead potentially shrinking in scope. Samsung, which had been playing catch-up in HBM4E qualification, arguably has less to lose from a lower-spec standard and may even see the bar to compete come down - which helps explain why its shares held up while SK Hynix's did not. The same headline produced opposite reactions because the two companies stood to gain or lose different things from it.

The foreign investor flow tells a related story. Early buying looked like bargain-hunting after the prior day's crash, and it pushed the index higher. Once the Nvidia HBM report spread around midday, that buying reversed into net selling of 856.2 billion won almost as quickly as it had arrived. Individual and institutional buying cushioned some of the fall but couldn't offset the scale of the foreign outflow - a pattern echoing August 5-6, when Kospi jumped 3.85% one day and crashed hard enough to trigger a sidecar the next, underscoring how fast foreign positioning can reverse and how much it can move an index this concentrated in two stocks.

The solar rally is a useful counterpoint showing how differently individual names can trade even on a red index day. The new tariffs take effect December 4 and apply additional duties to polysilicon derivatives priced below a US-set floor, plus a 15% tariff on some products - effectively targeting China's dominant polysilicon supply chain. Hanwha Solutions, with a Georgia production base, and OCI Holdings, with non-China polysilicon sourcing, stand to gain a relative cost advantage over Chinese competitors now facing that tariff burden, and the market priced that in immediately. On a day when a single HBM rumor rattled chip stocks, a single trade-policy decision sent an entirely different sector up double digits.

The day's whipsaw also makes more sense against the backdrop of the US calendar. The July nonfarm payrolls report was due out that night, Korea time, with Dow Jones-surveyed economists split between forecasts near 80,000 jobs and other estimates as low as the 20,000s - a wide range reflecting genuine uncertainty. The prior ADP private payrolls report had already missed expectations. Ahead of a macro release with that much riding on it, investors tend to react more sharply than usual to smaller, company-specific news as they trim and adjust positions, and that dynamic likely amplified the afternoon's swing.

## What to Take Away From This

- **The same chip-sector headline can help one company and hurt another.** Nvidia's reported HBM spec review was bad news for SK Hynix's frontrunner premium but arguably neutral-to-positive for Samsung's catch-up position. Don't read "chip stocks fell" as a single story - ask what each company specifically stood to gain or lose.
- **A morning rally can fully reverse before the close.** Kospi's swing from +1.83% to -0.60% shows the risk of chasing an intraday move based only on where the index stood at a single point. Watching net buying/selling by investor type intraday helps gauge how fragile a rally is.
- **A red index day can still hide double-digit winners.** Kospi fell 0.60% while solar names jumped over 20%. Don't judge a trading day purely by the headline index number - check whether news specific to your own holdings or sector moved independently of it.
- **Trade policy can move a sector overnight, unconnected to earnings.** The solar tariff illustrates how a government policy shift can create sharply different winners and losers based on supply-chain geography (local production, non-China sourcing) rather than anything a company itself did.
- **Volatility tends to rise ahead of major macro releases.** With forecasts for the US jobs report split widely and the prior ADP print already weak, markets were primed to overreact to smaller company-specific news - a pattern worth remembering the next time a big data release is on the calendar.

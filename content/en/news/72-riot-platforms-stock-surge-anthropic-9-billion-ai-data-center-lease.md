## What Happened

After the closing bell on Monday, August 10, bitcoin miner Riot Platforms (Nasdaq: RIOT) reported second-quarter 2026 results alongside a deal that reshapes what kind of company it's becoming. Riot announced a 20-year lease for 191 megawatts of critical IT capacity at its Rockdale, Texas campus with what it called a "frontier AI lab" - multiple outlets reported the counterparty is Anthropic - worth roughly $9.1 billion in total contract revenue over the lease term. Shares spiked as much as 25% in after-hours trading on the news, then extended those gains into premarket the next morning, trading up 17-19% as the broader market digested the announcement.

The underlying quarterly numbers also beat expectations. Second-quarter revenue came in at $174.24 million, up from $152.99 million a year earlier and about 17% ahead of the Zacks consensus estimate. On the bottom line, though, Riot swung to a net loss of $237.17 million, reversing a net income of $219.45 million in the same quarter last year. Diluted loss per share was $0.68, compared with diluted EPS of $0.58 a year ago. That swing to a loss largely reflects accounting factors tied to rising mining costs and asset revaluations rather than a collapse in the core business - and it was overshadowed almost entirely by investor enthusiasm for the new AI leasing story.

The terms of the lease are what set this apart from a routine earnings beat. The base term runs through June 2048, with two five-year extension options that could push total potential contract value to $16.1 billion if both are exercised. Riot estimates cumulative net operating income of $7.3-8.2 billion over the base term, or roughly $365-411 million a year on average. The initial 96 megawatts are slated to come online in December 2027, with the full 191 megawatts deployed by June 2028. Morgan Stanley is providing a $573 million interim financing facility to cover early development costs, with plans to transition to investment-grade, credit-backed long-term financing later. Combined with an AMD lease signed at the same Rockdale campus back in January, Riot now has 241 megawatts of IT capacity under contract at that single site.

## Why a Bitcoin Miner Is Pivoting Toward AI Data Centers

To understand why Riot made this move, it helps to look at the structural squeeze bitcoin mining has been under. Every halving cuts mining rewards in half, while global competition keeps pushing network-wide difficulty higher as more mining capacity comes online. The result is that the power and capital required to mine a single bitcoin keeps rising over time, even as revenue remains tied almost entirely to one volatile input: the bitcoin price. Riot's hashrate stood at roughly 42.5 EH/s, and despite full-year 2025 revenue of $647 million on 5,686 bitcoin mined, the company still posted a $663 million net loss for the year - a reminder that scale alone doesn't guarantee profitability in this business.

An AI data center lease offers a fundamentally different revenue model. Where bitcoin mining revenue swings daily with the coin price, a 20-year lease like this one locks in fixed rental income for the life of the contract. The $365-411 million in average annual net operating income Riot has already modeled is a cash flow that, as long as the lease terms hold, doesn't move with bitcoin's price swings. In effect, Riot is repurposing power infrastructure and land it already controls from its mining operations to reduce the volatility of its overall business and change how the market values the company. That's part of a broader shift: investors increasingly view large-scale miners less as pure "bitcoin proxies" and more as owners of power infrastructure with a credible path into AI data centers.

That reframing showed up across the sector on the same day - shares of fellow bitcoin miners IREN, Applied Digital, and TeraWulf all rose in sympathy with Riot's announcement, reflecting a shared thesis that companies sitting on large power contracts and data-center-ready land are candidates for similar AI leasing deals. Wall Street's reaction has leaned favorable too: Morgan Stanley carries an Overweight rating on Riot with a $36 price target, while Citi raised its target from $21 to $28 while maintaining a Buy rating.

Risks remain, though. It will take until late 2027 for even the initial capacity to generate revenue, and full deployment isn't expected until mid-2028 - a long runway during which AI data center demand could soften or the tenant's credit profile could change. Riot also still carries substantial revenue and losses from its core bitcoin mining operations, meaning this single lease, however large, doesn't immediately transform the company's overall earnings picture.

## What to Take Away From This

- **Markets often react more to tomorrow's cash flow than today's earnings.** Riot swung to a net loss, yet its stock spiked as much as 25% in after-hours trading purely on the strength of a 20-year contract story. When reading an earnings report, watch how new deal or contract disclosures move the stock independently of the income statement's current-quarter numbers.
- **When a company whose revenue depends on a volatile commodity or coin price locks in a long-term fixed contract, the entire character of its valuation can shift.** Adding a 20-year lease income stream to a high-volatility mining business is prompting the market to re-rate Riot as an infrastructure owner rather than a pure mining play - a dynamic similar to how [Datadog got re-rated on its broadening AI customer base even after a largest-customer scare](/en/news/datadog-stock-rebound-openai-usage-cut-analyst-price-targets/): in both cases, a structural change in the business, not just a single quarter's numbers, drove the re-rating.
- **A major deal from a sector leader tends to lift peers too.** IREN, Applied Digital, and TeraWulf all rallied alongside Riot the same day. When a single stock has a big catalyst, it's worth checking whether other companies with similar business models are moving in sympathy - that often signals a sector-wide reassessment, not just a single-name story.
- **Total contract value and actual near-term cash flow are two different numbers.** The $9.1 billion headline figure is recognized over 20 years, and revenue only starts flowing once capacity comes online in late 2027. When evaluating deal announcements, look past the total contract size to when revenue and earnings are actually expected to show up.
- **Companies mid-pivot between two business models carry the risk profile of both.** Riot still books meaningful mining-related losses even as its AI leasing story develops. Investing in a company shifting from crypto mining to AI infrastructure means tracking both the credibility of the new contracts and the ongoing performance of the legacy business.

## What Happened

On Thursday, October 1, chipmaker onsemi (Nasdaq: ON) announced a major revision to its planned acquisition of semiconductor design firm Synaptics (Nasdaq: SYNA). The original deal, struck back in June, was an all-stock transaction worth roughly $7 billion, under which Synaptics shareholders would receive 1.350 shares of onsemi stock for every Synaptics share they held. The revised deal scraps the stock swap entirely in favor of $123 per share in cash, shrinking the headline deal value to about $5.7 billion. The market reacted immediately and strongly: onsemi shares jumped 5-8% that day, while Synaptics shares surged 14.08% to close at $121.10. The roughly 1.6% gap between that closing price and the $123 deal price is a textbook merger-arbitrage spread - the market pricing in the remaining time and regulatory risk before the deal actually closes.

What makes this genuinely interesting is that the reaction is the mirror image of what happened the first time around. When onsemi first announced the stock-for-stock deal back on June 25, its own shares fell roughly 6% in after-hours trading, even as Synaptics jumped about 13% on the news. Same two companies, same strategic rationale, but simply restructuring how the deal gets paid flipped onsemi's own stock reaction from a decline to a rally.

## Why the Deal Structure Changed: A Competing Bidder Showed Up

The catalyst behind the revision was an unsolicited competing proposal - an undisclosed third party approached Synaptics with its own acquisition offer, putting pressure on onsemi to sweeten its terms or risk losing the deal entirely. The company's solution was, on its face, a little counterintuitive: it lowered the headline deal value from $7 billion to $5.7 billion while simultaneously making the offer more attractive to Synaptics shareholders. The key was switching to cash. In a stock-for-stock deal, the value a target's shareholders ultimately receive fluctuates with the acquirer's own stock price between announcement and closing. In a cash deal, $123 per share is locked in regardless of what happens to onsemi's stock in the meantime. With a rival bidder in the picture, that certainty became a powerful selling point - onsemi was effectively telling the Synaptics board and its shareholders that its offer carried materially less risk than a stock-based alternative.

The financing mechanics are worth noting too. Onsemi plans to fund the cash purchase using a combination of cash on hand and $2.45 billion in newly committed senior secured term loans from Morgan Stanley. This financing choice has a second benefit beyond winning over Synaptics shareholders: it avoids diluting onsemi's own existing shareholders. Under the original stock deal, onsemi would have had to issue new shares to pay for the acquisition, shrinking the ownership percentage represented by every existing onsemi share. With cash replacing stock, that dilution risk disappears, and the company said the deal is now expected to be immediately accretive to non-GAAP earnings per share upon closing. Management also pointed to an estimated $200 million in cost synergies within 18 months of the deal closing.

## The Strategic Logic: Building a "Physical AI" Platform

To understand why onsemi wants Synaptics in the first place, it helps to look at what each company brings to the table. Onsemi is an established leader in power management semiconductors and sensors, particularly in automotive and industrial markets. Synaptics, meanwhile, brings on-device AI processing chips along with wireless connectivity technology - WiFi, Bluetooth, and GPS. Combined, the two portfolios form a more complete platform: sensors that perceive the physical environment, on-device chips that process and decide in real time, wireless connectivity to communicate that data, and efficient power management to run it all. This is the "physical AI" or "edge AI" thesis that chip industry executives have increasingly rallied around - AI that doesn't run in a cloud data center processing large language model queries, but instead lives inside autonomous vehicles, industrial robots, and wearable devices, making real-time decisions locally. Onsemi has said the acquisition is expected to expand its total addressable market by roughly $30 billion, reaching an estimated $243 billion by 2030.

Viewed through that lens, this isn't simply a bargain-hunting acquisition - it's a signal of where the broader AI investment theme is heading next. While Nvidia and AMD duke it out over data-center AI accelerators, companies like onsemi with deep roots in automotive and industrial chips are using M&A to stake out ground in the comparatively less-crowded edge AI space before it becomes the next battleground.

## The Deal Isn't Closed Yet

The transaction still requires approval from Synaptics shareholders and clearance from regulators, and both companies are targeting a mid-2027 closing. In other words, today's stock pop reflects the market's judgment that the deal is now more likely to go through on favorable terms - not that it's a done deal. As noted above, the roughly 1.6% gap between Synaptics' $121.10 close and the $123 agreed price captures that remaining uncertainty: the risk of a regulatory snag, a delay, or another twist before closing. If the process proceeds smoothly, expect that spread to narrow as the closing date approaches; if a new complication emerges - a regulatory objection, another competing bid - expect it to widen again, along with renewed volatility in both stocks.

## What to Take Away From This

- **The same acquisition can produce opposite stock reactions for the acquirer depending on whether it's paid in stock or cash.** Stock deals often raise dilution concerns that weigh on the acquirer's own share price, while cash deals - especially ones framed as immediately earnings-accretive - can instead push the acquirer's stock higher.
- **When a target's shares trade below the agreed cash deal price, that spread is a direct readout of how confident the market is that the deal closes as planned.** Synaptics trading at $121.10 against a $123 deal price signals the market sees the deal as likely, but not yet certain.
- **A disclosure mentioning an "unsolicited" competing proposal is a signal that the original acquirer is under pressure to improve its terms.** These competitive bidding situations frequently work in favor of the target company's shareholders.
- **In semiconductor M&A, it's worth distinguishing data-center AI deals from edge/physical AI deals.** Separate from the fierce competition among Nvidia, AMD, and others for cloud AI chip dominance, chipmakers with automotive and industrial roots - like onsemi - are increasingly using acquisitions to build out edge AI platforms.

## FAQ

### Why did onsemi stock fall in June but rise in October on essentially the same deal?
In June, the deal was structured as an all-stock transaction, meaning onsemi would issue new shares to pay Synaptics shareholders - a structure that raised dilution concerns among existing onsemi investors and sent the stock down about 6% in after-hours trading. In October, the deal switched to an all-cash structure at $123 per share, eliminating that dilution risk, and the company said the revised deal would be immediately accretive to earnings - sending the stock up 5-8% instead.

### Is this acquisition finalized?
Not yet. It still needs approval from Synaptics shareholders and clearance from regulators, with both companies targeting a mid-2027 close. The fact that Synaptics trades slightly below the $123 deal price ($121.10) reflects that remaining uncertainty.

### What exactly is "physical AI" or "edge AI"?
Unlike cloud AI, which runs large language models in data centers, physical or edge AI refers to the chips and systems embedded directly in devices - autonomous vehicles, industrial robots, wearables - that need to sense their surroundings and make decisions in real time without relying on a remote server. Combining onsemi's power management and sensor technology with Synaptics' on-device AI chips and wireless connectivity is meant to build exactly that kind of integrated edge AI platform.

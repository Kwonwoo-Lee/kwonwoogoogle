## What Happened

Before the opening bell on Tuesday, October 27, fintech company SoFi Technologies (NASDAQ: SOFI) reported third-quarter earnings, and the stock went on to surge as much as 15% intraday - from a prior close of $14.46 to roughly $16.60 at its peak - before settling into a close up around 13%. For a stock that had fallen as much as 45% at points during 2026, making it one of the worst-performing names among this year's fintech cohort, that's a single trading day clawing back a meaningful chunk of the year's damage.

The numbers behind the move were genuinely strong across the board. Adjusted EPS came in at $0.18, above the $0.17 consensus estimate, while adjusted net revenue hit $1.37 billion - nearly 10% above the roughly $1.25-$1.26 billion Wall Street had modeled. Adjusted EBITDA, the company's key profitability metric, jumped 70% year over year to a record $470 million. On the lending side, quarterly loan originations - the core engine of SoFi's business - hit a record $16.9 billion, up 71% year over year, while the company added 1.2 million new members in the quarter, its largest single-quarter gain ever. Total membership now stands at 17.1 million, up 35% from a year ago.

SoFi didn't stop at beating the quarter. It raised full-year 2026 guidance to roughly $5.04 billion in adjusted net revenue and $1.66 billion in adjusted EBITDA. More notably, it issued first-ever guidance for 2027: approximately $6.4 billion in adjusted net revenue, $2.24 billion in adjusted EBITDA, and $0.90 in EPS. That's not just "this quarter was good" - it's management handing the market a specific, multi-year roadmap for the first time.

## Why the Reaction Was This Dramatic

To understand the size of this move, you need to understand where SoFi's stock stood before it happened. The shares had fallen as much as 45% at points in 2026, making the stock one of the year's weakest fintech performers. Part of that was a pattern specific to SoFi: over recent quarters, the company had repeatedly beaten earnings estimates only to see the stock sell off anyway - a classic "sell-on-beat" dynamic driven by conservative guidance or soft spots in specific segments, like client attrition in its Technology Platform business. That track record left Wall Street deeply split. Some banks held Underweight ratings with price targets in the $16-$18 range, while others had Overweight calls with targets above $30 - a mean target around $21 masking a genuinely wide disagreement about where this stock was headed.

This quarter mattered because it answered that skepticism with numbers that were hard to wave away. A 70% jump in EBITDA isn't just "revenue grew" - it signals that SoFi is scaling top-line growth and profitability at the same time, something many fintechs struggle to do simultaneously (plenty grow members or transaction volume while staying unprofitable). SoFi grew loan originations 71% while also posting record profitability in the same quarter, which reads as evidence that its core model - using its bank charter to gather cheap deposits and deploy them into loans - is now generating real economies of scale. The 35% jump in membership matters for the same reason: as long as customer acquisition costs stay in check, a bigger member base is a bigger future funnel for cross-selling loans, deposits, and investment products.

Second, and arguably more important, was the first-ever 2027 guide. For growth stocks - especially a still-volatile fintech name like SoFi - the thing investors fear most is uncertainty about whether a good quarter is a one-off or part of a durable trend. Without a clear basis for judging that, markets apply a valuation discount. By laying out specific revenue, EBITDA, and EPS targets through 2027, management signaled real conviction in its own growth trajectory. The fact that this broke the stock's old sell-on-beat pattern - rather than extending it - tells you the market chose, this time, to read the guidance as an upside signal rather than a downside risk.

Finally, SoFi's expanding push into crypto and blockchain infrastructure forms part of the backdrop. The company became the first nationally chartered bank to offer consumer crypto trading and has issued its own stablecoin, SoFiUSD, on a public blockchain - part of a broader strategy of layering digital-asset products on top of its traditional banking license. Its blockchain-based international remittance service now covers more than 30 countries. These newer businesses still contribute a relatively small slice of total revenue, but investors increasingly view them as evidence that SoFi is diversifying its growth story beyond its original identity as a student-loan refinancing company. With the Fed widely expected to hold rates steady at its October 28 meeting, any reduction in rate-path uncertainty is also a tailwind for a lending-heavy fintech bank like SoFi.

## What to Take Away From This

- **A beat and a stock's reaction to it are two separate questions.** SoFi had beaten estimates in recent quarters only to see shares fall anyway. Whether a number clears consensus and whether it actually resolves the specific doubts the market has been holding are different things - if you see a repeated pattern of "beats but the stock drops anyway," dig into what the market is actually worried about before assuming the next beat will behave differently.
- **A multi-year guide can be a bigger catalyst than the quarter itself.** It wasn't this quarter's numbers alone that drove the size of the move - it was the first-ever 2027 target. For a volatile growth name, a credible multi-year roadmap can unwind an uncertainty discount in a single session.
- **Check whether growth and profitability are improving together.** Loan originations (scale) and EBITDA (profitability) both hit records in the same quarter, which is what made this beat higher-quality than a typical top-line-only surprise. One without the other tends to leave the "growing but not yet profitable" doubt intact.
- **A wide spread in analyst targets is itself a volatility signal.** Price targets ranging from $16 to above $30 meant Wall Street had no real consensus on direction. Stocks with that kind of split tend to see outsized moves around binary catalysts like earnings - worth flagging before the print, not just explaining after.

## FAQ

### Did SoFi's 15% jump erase its 2026 losses?

No. With the stock down as much as 45% at points this year, a single-day gain in the mid-teens percentage range doesn't come close to recovering the full year-to-date decline. It's more accurate to read this move as the first real step in rebuilding market trust - resolving a meaningful chunk of accumulated skepticism - rather than a full reversal of 2026's losses.

### Why didn't SoFi's usual "sell-on-beat" pattern repeat this time?

In prior quarters, SoFi's stock drops typically traced back to guidance or specific segment concerns (like Technology Platform softness) rather than the headline numbers themselves. This time, both the current quarter (EPS, revenue, and EBITDA all beat) and forward guidance (a raised 2026 outlook plus a brand-new 2027 target) came through positive at once, leaving investors with little of the "good quarter, bad signal underneath" dynamic that had weighed on the stock before.

### How much is SoFi's crypto and blockchain business actually contributing to these results?

Still a relatively small share of total revenue - the core driver of this quarter's beat remains lending and financial services growth. That said, crypto trading, SoFi's own stablecoin, and its blockchain-based remittance network support a broader narrative that SoFi is evolving from a student-loan-focused lender into a diversified digital financial platform, which appears to be part of why investors re-rated the stock's longer-term growth story so sharply.

## What Happened

U.S. stocks rallied hard on Tuesday, August 4, with Palantir Technologies (PLTR) as the clear catalyst.

- **S&P 500**: up 1.79% to a record 7,737
- **Dow Jones**: up 907 points (1.71%) to a record 54,086
- **Nasdaq Composite**: up 2.59% to 26,585

Palantir reported Q2 revenue of $1.94 billion, up 93% year-over-year and well above the $1.8 billion analysts expected. Adjusted EPS came in at $0.41 versus a $0.35 estimate. The company also raised its full-year revenue guidance from roughly $7.65-7.66 billion to $8.16 billion. Shares jumped as much as nearly 30% intraday - the stock's best day in about a year.

Elsewhere, Caterpillar (CAT) rose 5.6% on record quarterly revenue, while Chipotle (CMG) fell nearly 10% after a salmonella outbreak tied to jalapeños at several Minnesota locations.

## Why It Matters - What's Actually Inside Palantir's Numbers

The composition of Palantir's growth is the real story here.

- **U.S. commercial revenue**: up 149% year-over-year
- **U.S. government revenue**: up 90% year-over-year, up 18% quarter-over-quarter

For a company long typecast as a government-contracts business, proving that commercial demand is now growing even faster than government demand is a meaningful shift - and the market treated it that way. CEO Alex Karp called the quarter "otherworldly," adding that "the sovereign AI revolution makes us very optimistic about the future."

It's also worth noting the rally wasn't confined to Palantir. Tech stocks broadly gained 4.20% and industrials gained 3.42% on the day, suggesting the market read this earnings report as evidence that AI infrastructure spending is starting to show up as real revenue - almost the opposite conclusion from the "is AI spending sustainable" doubt that drove last week's semiconductor selloff (see our [earlier piece](/en/news/semiconductor-selloff-china-ai/) on that).

## What to Take Away From This

- **Look at the composition of an earnings beat, not just the headline number.** A 93% revenue growth headline is impressive, but it was the split between commercial (149%) and government (90%) growth that told the market this business is genuinely diversifying - and that's what drove the size of the reaction.
- **A single stock's earnings can move an entire sector.** In narrative-driven groups like AI/tech, a strong print from a bellwether name can lift stocks with no direct business connection to it. The same mechanism works in reverse, as last week's semiconductor selloff showed.
- **Beating estimates alone rarely explains a 30% move - raised guidance usually does the heavy lifting.** The size of this rally reflects not just that Palantir beat expectations, but that management convinced the market the growth is durable enough to raise full-year guidance on top of it.

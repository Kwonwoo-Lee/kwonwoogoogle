## What Happened

Anthropic's stock began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT on Thursday, November 19 - one day after its IPO priced at $83 a share on Wednesday evening. That $83 print was already notable on its own: it landed five dollars above the top of the $70-to-$78 range Anthropic had marketed just over a week earlier, and the roughly 980-million-share offering raised about $81.3 billion, surpassing SpaceX's $75 billion deal from this past June as the largest IPO on record (background: [Anthropic Prices IPO at $83, Raises $81.3 Billion](/en/news/anthropic-ipo-price-83-dollars-81-billion-record-spacex/)).

But the number investors actually cared about on Thursday morning wasn't the offer price - it was the opening trade. Newly listed stocks don't start changing hands the moment the 9:30 a.m. bell rings. Instead, Nasdaq's designated market maker collects buy and sell interest over a window of time and clears the very first trade through a single-price "opening cross," matching supply against demand at one number. Until that cross actually happens, any price quoted is just an indication, not a trade - and those indications moved around plenty on Thursday morning. Live blogs at Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance flagged pre-open indications climbing as high as $104 to $110, implying a 25% to 33% premium over the $83 offer price.

The actual print came in lower than that. According to 24/7 Wall St., Anthropic's shares opened trading at $99.60 - exactly 20% above the $83 IPO price. That's below the hottest indications floated earlier that morning, but it's still a sizable first-trade pop by any normal standard. Run that $99.60 figure through the same fully diluted math used to calculate Anthropic's roughly $1.5 trillion valuation at the $83 offer price, and the implied valuation at the open jumps to somewhere in the neighborhood of $1.8 trillion. Forbes reported that Nasdaq futures were up as much as 2% that morning on anticipation of the debut, and NBC News noted the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite climbed as much as 1.9% in early trading, with some of that enthusiasm tied directly to Anthropic's listing.

## Why the Comparison to SpaceX Is the More Telling Number

A 20% premium over the offer price sounds impressive in isolation, but the figure that actually puts it in perspective comes from the IPO Anthropic just dethroned: SpaceX's.

When SpaceX went public on June 12, it priced at $135 a share and opened at $150 - an 11% pop. From there, the stock ran as high as $176.52 intraday, a 31% gain, before giving back much of that move to close the day at $160.95, up 19% from the offer price. In other words, SpaceX needed an entire trading session - open, intraday swings, and all - to produce a 19% first-day gain. Anthropic matched and then exceeded that same 19% figure at the opening trade alone, before a single minute of regular intraday trading had even occurred. On that narrow measure, Anthropic's debut generated more heat at the starting gun than SpaceX's did across its whole first day.

That comparison comes with an important caveat, though, and SpaceX's own chart supplies it: intraday highs do not reliably hold into the close. SpaceX gave back roughly 12 percentage points of gain between its 31% intraday peak and its 19% closing print. Anthropic's own morning already showed a similar pattern in miniature - indications near $104-$110 cooled to an actual opening trade of $99.60. Every number quoted on an IPO's debut day - the pre-open indication, the opening print, the intraday high, the closing price - is a snapshot of a different moment, and conflating any one of them with "how much the stock gained today" risks comparing numbers that were never measuring the same thing. Whether Anthropic's own closing price, once the session ends, lands above or below that $99.60 opening print is simply not yet known as of this writing.

The mechanics behind that opening print matter here, too. During the pre-open window, only limit orders in whole-share lots are accepted - market orders are rejected until after the cross - which is exactly why indicative quotes can swing meaningfully before the first real trade happens. For an offering this large (roughly 980 million public shares, plus a separate $26 billion private placement bringing the combined deal closer to $107 billion), that price-discovery window tends to run longer than usual, and Thursday's actual first trade came well after the standard 9:30 a.m. opening bell.

The valuation math is worth revisiting at this new price, too. The $1.5 trillion figure attached to the $83 offer price was calculated on a fully diluted basis - every stock option, restricted share, and the newly issued non-voting Class N shares counted as if already converted into ordinary stock. Apply that same fully diluted share count (implied at roughly 18.1 billion shares) to the $99.60 opening price, and the resulting valuation comes out to roughly $1.8 trillion - a number that moves in lockstep with the share price, not a fixed figure investors can treat as settled. Layer on the fact that a meaningful slice of demand came through the $26 billion private placement from chip and cloud suppliers like Nvidia, Google, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC, and it becomes harder to say how much of Thursday's pop reflects pure market appetite versus strategic investors with a commercial stake in Anthropic's success.

## What to Take Away From This

- **An opening print is a starting point for volatility, not a final grade on the IPO.** Anthropic's open already topped SpaceX's full first-day gain, but SpaceX's own session shows how much ground a stock can give up - or add - between the open and the close.
- **A pre-open indication and an actual trade are two different things.** The $104-$110 range floated Thursday morning never became a real trade; the actual opening print was $99.60. Always check whether a quoted IPO-day number reflects an indication or a filled order.
- **Fully diluted valuations move with the share price, so they need to be recalculated, not memorized.** The jump from a $1.5 trillion valuation at the $83 offer price to roughly $1.8 trillion at the $99.60 open is the same math applied to a new number, not a new calculation.
- **Match your time frame when comparing pop sizes across different IPOs.** Open-to-offer, close-to-offer, and intraday-high-to-offer are three different metrics; comparing a number measured one way for one company against a number measured a different way for another produces a misleading conclusion.
- **Heavy strategic-investor participation can dampen first-day volatility while also clouding the demand signal.** When suppliers and commercial partners hold a large chunk of a deal, as they do here, an early price pop is a weaker proxy for broad market conviction than it would otherwise be.

## FAQ

### Why didn't Anthropic start trading right at 9:30 a.m.?
Newly listed stocks don't open through continuous trading the way already-listed stocks do. Nasdaq's designated market maker collects buy and sell orders over a window of time and settles the first trade through a single-price "opening cross" once supply and demand line up. Larger, more heavily subscribed offerings - like this one - tend to take longer to clear that process, which is why the first trade landed well after the standard opening bell.

### Is a 20% opening gain a strong result or a disappointing one?
It depends on which baseline you use. Measured against the $83 IPO price, it's a clear pop that already exceeds the 19% gain SpaceX produced across its entire first trading day. Measured against the hottest pre-open indications floated that same morning ($104-$110, implying a 25%-33% premium), the actual opening print came in noticeably lower. Both framings are accurate; they're just answering different questions.

### How was the roughly $1.8 trillion valuation calculated?
It's the $99.60 opening price multiplied by Anthropic's fully diluted share count - meaning every stock option, restricted share, and the newly created non-voting Class N share is counted as if already converted into ordinary stock, for an implied total of roughly 18.1 billion shares. That's the same method used to calculate the roughly $1.5 trillion figure tied to the $83 offer price; only the share price input has changed.

### Should I buy ANT right around the opening price?
There's no universal answer. What SpaceX's debut demonstrates is that first-day prices for large, high-profile IPOs can swing substantially between the open, the intraday high, and the close, so the opening trade is not a reliable floor or ceiling. Anyone considering a position would be well served to also look at the deal's lock-up expiration schedule, the proportion of demand tied to strategic rather than purely financial investors, and how the fully diluted valuation compares across the specific basis being used before making a decision.

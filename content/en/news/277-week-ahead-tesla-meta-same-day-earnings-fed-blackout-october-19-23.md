## What Happened

Starting at midnight Eastern on Saturday, October 17, the Federal Reserve entered its "blackout period" — the stretch before an FOMC meeting during which the Fed Chair, governors, and regional bank presidents stop making any public comments about monetary policy. The rule kicks in at midnight on the second Saturday before a meeting and runs through the day after it. With the next FOMC meeting set for October 27-28, that means the blackout covers October 17 through October 29 — and this entire week (October 19-23) falls squarely inside it. The last word investors got was from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh on Friday, October 16, who stuck to boilerplate language about prioritizing price stability without tipping his hand on the rate path. Markets now have to wait until the October 28 FOMC statement and press conference for the next real signal.

Into that vacuum steps a cluster of mega-cap earnings. The single biggest event is Wednesday, October 21, after the close, when Tesla (Nasdaq: TSLA) and Meta Platforms (Nasdaq: META) report Q3 results on the same day, in the same window. Tesla already gave markets a preview on October 2, when it disclosed Q3 global deliveries of 486,532 vehicles — about 5% (24,558 units) above the company-compiled analyst consensus of 461,974. That beat still came in 2.1% below the all-time quarterly record of 497,099 set in Q3 2025, when U.S. buyers rushed to lock in the $7,500 federal EV tax credit before it expired on September 30, 2025. Energy storage deployments of 13.7 gigawatt-hours (GWh) landed below the 15.9 GWh analysts had expected. Meta confirmed its October 21 earnings date in a press release on October 12, with a conference call scheduled for 1:30pm Pacific / 4:30pm Eastern.

The rest of the week is just as packed. GE Aerospace (NYSE: GE) reports before the bell Monday, October 19. Tuesday, October 20 brings Verizon and Coca-Cola before the open, plus Netflix after the close. The day after Tesla and Meta report, Thursday, October 22 before the open, Procter & Gamble (NYSE: PG) posts fiscal Q1 2027 results; Wall Street expects earnings per share of roughly $1.89, down about 5% from the year-ago quarter's $1.93. The week wraps up Friday, October 23, with S&P Global's flash October manufacturing and services PMI readings, alongside the final October University of Michigan consumer sentiment figure and September's Conference Board Leading Economic Index.

## Why This Week Boils Down to "Big Tech Earnings Filling a Fed-Shaped Gap"

To understand why this particular combination matters, look at what just happened the week before. The Nasdaq Composite fell 4.5% last week, its worst weekly showing since June, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) logged an eight-session losing streak — its longest since 2006. Over that same stretch, bond yields on notes issued by AI cloud providers CoreWeave and Nebius spiked and credit default swaps flashed distress signals, spreading "AI data-center debt" anxiety across the broader market. That selloff was concentrated in semiconductors and AI infrastructure names specifically, not the market as a whole — which is exactly why this week's Tesla and Meta results carry outsized weight: investors want to know whether these results extend that selective selloff or help calm it.

Meta sits at the center of the capex-anxiety debate in particular. Since the CoreWeave/Nebius credit scare, the question markets keep returning to is how Big Tech is financing its AI data-center buildout, and when that spending actually turns into profit. If Meta raises its already-elevated capital expenditure guidance for next year without clearly tying it to revenue growth, it risks reigniting the same "AI debt overhang" worry that just rattled credit markets. If instead Meta can show advertising growth and AI spending efficiency in hard numbers, it could help trigger a rotation of capital back out of distressed AI-infrastructure names and into mega-cap platforms with proven cash flow. Tesla's story runs on a different track: having already confirmed the delivery beat, attention shifts to the pace of its robotaxi rollout, the profitability of its energy storage business, and fourth-quarter guidance. Options markets have reportedly priced in a post-earnings move of roughly plus-or-minus 6% for Tesla shares — in line with the volatility the stock has shown around earnings in recent years.

The Fed's silence compounds the stakes. Central bank commentary normally works as a kind of shock absorber, letting officials nudge market rate expectations in real time between data points. This week, that mechanism is simply switched off until the October 28 statement. That leaves price action — stock moves, bond yields, credit spreads — and hard data (the PMIs, consumer sentiment) to do all the work of setting market direction on their own. Given that September's CPI reading had already come in hotter than expected and pushed up the odds of an October rate hike, a data-light, Fed-silent week like this one carries real potential for markets to overcorrect expectations that were already running hot.

Crypto markets round out the backdrop worth watching. Bitcoin has absorbed more than $1 billion in net outflows from spot ETFs so far in October and is defending support in the low-$81,000s — roughly 35% below its all-time high of $126,198. Because bitcoin is often treated as a leading indicator of risk appetite, whether that support level holds through this week's earnings crush is itself a useful read on investor sentiment heading into the Fed's return to the microphone next week.

Netflix's earnings, due Tuesday, October 20, offer an indirect reference point too — [Netflix is reporting near a 52-week low even as Wall Street's average price target still sits around $90](/en/news/netflix-q3-2026-earnings-preview-stock-near-52-week-low/), highlighting the gap between what the stock price has already priced in and what analysts expect further out. A similar gap could show up in how Tesla and Meta trade this week relative to their own long-run analyst targets, which is why grouping this week's mega-cap reports together reveals a shared theme: tension between short-term price action and longer-term valuation views.

## What to Take Away From This

- **A central bank's "silent period" is exactly when other catalysts — earnings, data — carry more weight than usual.** During a week with no Fed commentary, markets can overreact to individual earnings or data releases, which is a good reason to size positions more conservatively.
- **Two very different mega-caps reporting on the same day can double as a rotation signal.** When companies with distinct business models — Tesla's EVs/robotaxis versus Meta's advertising/AI infrastructure — report together, the relative difference in how each stock reacts offers a clue about which investment theme is in favor at that moment.
- **Pre-released operating metrics and the full earnings report aren't the same event.** When a company like Tesla discloses quarterly deliveries ahead of its earnings date, it's worth distinguishing how much of that news the stock has already priced in versus what's still left to react to once full financials and guidance arrive.

## FAQ

### Why does it matter that Tesla and Meta report on the same day?
The two companies operate in completely different industries — electric vehicles and autonomous driving versus social media and AI infrastructure — but both carry growth stories built on heavy capital spending. Because they report simultaneously, investors can directly compare how the market treats each one, which offers a read on how much confidence the market currently has in large-scale tech and AI investment generally.

### How do markets gauge rate expectations when the Fed can't comment?
Instead of relying on officials' public remarks, markets lean on futures-based tools like the CME FedWatch tool to estimate the probability of rate hikes or cuts. During a quiet week like this one, those probabilities can swing more sharply around each new data release, from PMI readings to consumer sentiment, since there's no Fed commentary to anchor expectations in between.

### Could strong Tesla or Meta earnings reverse the semiconductor sector's eight-day losing streak?
Not necessarily. The chip-stock selloff was driven by a specific concern — worry about how AI data-center operators are financing their debt — so strong results from Tesla or Meta wouldn't automatically resolve credit-market anxiety that's specific to AI infrastructure financing. That said, if Meta's numbers demonstrate real efficiency in AI spending, it could at least partially restore confidence in AI investment as a theme more broadly.

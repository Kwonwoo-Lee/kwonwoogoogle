## What Happened

Dick's Sporting Goods (NYSE: DKS), the largest sporting goods retailer in the United States, plunged 30.7% on Tuesday, August 25, closing at $124.32, down $55.01 from Monday's close of $179.33. It marked the company's worst single-session percentage decline on record - surpassing the roughly 24% post-earnings drop it suffered in August 2023 - sent shares to a two-year low, and extended a brutal run in which the stock has fallen in 10 of its last 11 trading sessions.

The trigger was Dick's second-quarter earnings report, released before the opening bell. Adjusted earnings per share came in at $3.53, missing the $3.78 consensus estimate by 25 cents and down roughly 26% year-over-year. Revenue landed at $5.59 billion versus the $5.65 billion analysts had expected - a modest miss on its own. What actually rattled the market was the full-year guidance cut that came with it. Dick's slashed its full-year adjusted EPS outlook to a range of $11.00 to $12.00, down from its prior guidance of $13.50 to $14.50 - roughly 19% below the $14.20 analyst consensus at the midpoint. Full-year revenue guidance was also lowered to a range of $21.90 billion to $22.20 billion, below the $22.35 billion consensus.

Management pointed to four factors behind the cut: an increasingly promotional retail environment across the industry, higher supply chain and fuel costs, a higher effective tax rate, and the drag from integrating Foot Locker, the footwear chain Dick's acquired for $2.4 billion in a deal completed last September. Of the four, Foot Locker drew the most scrutiny. On a pro forma basis, the Foot Locker segment posted a 3.6% decline in comparable sales for the quarter and an operating loss of $31.9 million - confirming that the newly acquired footwear network is dragging down group profitability in its very first fiscal year under Dick's ownership, rather than adding to it.

## Why This Matters: The Paradox of a "Modest Miss, Massive Reaction"

What makes this case notable is that Dick's didn't report a catastrophic quarter. The EPS miss was a mere 25 cents and revenue landed close to expectations. Yet the stock still lost nearly a third of its value in a single session. That gap exists because markets price a stock primarily on the earnings trajectory implied by forward guidance, not on the quarter just completed. Valuation multiples are built by discounting future cash flows, so cutting full-year EPS guidance by roughly 19% mechanically removes a large chunk of the earnings a stock's price was built on - a much bigger deal than a one-quarter shortfall of a few cents. For a retailer like Dick's, long viewed by investors as a stable, reliable cash generator rather than a high-growth story, a guidance cut of this size can hit even harder than it would for a growth stock, because the stability premium priced into the shares gets re-rated all at once.

The Foot Locker story is also a useful case study in how the synergy math pitched at deal announcement can diverge from what actually plays out during integration. When Dick's announced the $2.4 billion acquisition, it framed the deal as a path to becoming a "global leader in sports retail," projecting $100-125 million in medium-term cost synergies from procurement efficiencies and direct sourcing. In practice, the company is now navigating declining operating margins in the very first fiscal year of ownership. Dick's itself had flagged that synergy realization would be "back-half weighted" and that Foot Locker's profitability recovery would also skew toward the second half of the year - so some integration friction was expected going in. What wasn't fully priced in was an industry-wide promotional wave hitting at the same time, adding a second headwind on top of an integration process that was already going to take time. Footwear and sneakers - Foot Locker's core category - happen to be an especially discount-prone segment right now, as brand partners including Nike work through inventory cycles alongside new product launches. Management's own language on the earnings call - that conditions across "portions of the athletic footwear and apparel marketplace became increasingly promotional" as the quarter progressed - points directly at this dynamic.

This isn't purely a single-stock story either. It fits a broader pattern showing up across US retail earnings this season: companies reporting numbers that are close to consensus, but where the market fixates on forward guidance instead. Similar to what played out with Target and Lowe's reporting on the same day last week, the headline number matters less than what it implies about consumer discretionary spending going forward. Discount-prone, non-essential categories like footwear and apparel appear to be where that spending pressure is showing up most visibly - consumers increasingly waiting for markdowns rather than buying at full price.

## What to Take Away From This

- **The size of an earnings miss and the size of the stock reaction don't have to match.** A 25-cent EPS miss produced a 30%+ stock decline here because the market reacted overwhelmingly to what forward guidance implied about the year ahead, not to the quarter that just closed.
- **Verify the synergy story an acquisition is sold on against actual post-close results.** Foot Locker was pitched as a long-term growth driver, but posted an operating loss in its first fiscal year under new ownership. Comparing deal-announcement projections against subsequent quarterly filings is a habit worth building.
- **Promotional intensity is a leading indicator of retail margins.** Revenue can hold up even as discounting eats into profitability, so tracking gross margin trends alongside top-line growth gives a fuller picture than revenue figures alone.
- **Comparing results across companies in the same sector helps separate company-specific risk from industry-wide risk.** Whether Dick's slide reflects Foot Locker-specific integration problems or a broader promotional environment across footwear and apparel retail should become clearer as Nike, other footwear brands, and competing retailers report their own results.

## FAQ

### Why did DKS stock fall 30%+ when the EPS miss was only 25 cents?
The much bigger driver was the full-year guidance cut, not the quarterly miss itself. Dick's lowered its adjusted EPS outlook from $13.50-$14.50 to $11.00-$12.00, roughly a 19% reduction at the midpoint versus the prior consensus. Since stock prices are built on expected future earnings rather than the quarter just reported, a guidance cut of this magnitude created far more selling pressure than the quarterly numbers alone would have.

### Why is the Foot Locker acquisition weighing so heavily on results?
Dick's completed its $2.4 billion acquisition of Foot Locker in September 2025, expecting synergies from stronger brand-partner leverage and procurement efficiencies. Instead, in its first fiscal year under Dick's ownership, Foot Locker posted a pro forma comparable-sales decline and a $31.9 million operating loss, meaning integration costs and industry-wide promotional pressure are showing up before any of the projected synergies. Management has said synergy realization is expected to be back-half weighted, suggesting more integration noise could continue in the near term.

### Could this signal trouble for Nike or other footwear and apparel stocks?
The direct read-through is limited, but Dick's management specifically cited intensifying promotions "across portions of the athletic footwear and apparel marketplace" - language that could apply industry-wide. Whether other footwear retailers and brands report similar margin pressure in their upcoming results is worth watching for confirmation.

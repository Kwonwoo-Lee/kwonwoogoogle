## What Happened

Bitcoin's whipsaw week wasn't finished after all. Less than 48 hours after Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks knocked the token down to roughly $77,700, Bitcoin clawed back part of that loss on Sunday, August 30, rising 1.5% to trade around $78,960 and pressing directly against the $79,000 resistance zone that has capped every rally attempt since late last week. Futures open interest across major derivatives venues stood at roughly $54.83 billion on Sunday, a sign that traders are still actively positioning around this level rather than sitting on the sidelines waiting for Monday's cash-equity open.

The rebound didn't come out of nowhere. Bitcoin has now spent nearly two weeks oscillating in a band between roughly $75,000 and $81,000, repeatedly testing both edges without a decisive break in either direction. It touched an intraday high above $81,000 on Friday, August 28 - its strongest level since May - before Warsh's Jackson Hole speech reset rate-hike expectations and triggered a wave of forced long liquidations that pulled the price back under $78,000 by Saturday morning. Sunday's bounce recovers a meaningful chunk of that pullback without erasing it entirely, leaving Bitcoin roughly 2.5% below Friday's peak and squarely inside the same range it has occupied since mid-August.

Ethereum moved in step, trading in the $2,400 to $2,440 range with the $2,400 level acting as a closely watched support line, while XRP held near $1.40. Both tokens remain up sharply over the trailing two weeks - Ethereum roughly 29% and XRP roughly 33% - a reminder that even with Sunday's modest bounce, the broader August rally across major cryptocurrencies is still largely intact. Bitcoin itself is still trading comfortably above its 20-day, 50-day, and 200-day moving averages, a technical picture that chart-focused traders generally read as an uptrend that has cooled rather than broken.

What's different about Sunday's move compared with the sharper swings earlier in the week is the absence of an obvious single catalyst. Friday's breakout above $81,000 was driven by a short squeeze tied to the Treasury's bond-buyback announcement; Saturday's reversal was driven by Warsh's speech and roughly $478 million to $547 million in liquidations, concentrated on the long side. Sunday's recovery, by contrast, looks more like normal weekend trading finding its footing after a volatile 72 hours - dip buyers stepping in near a support zone that has held multiple times this month, without a fresh headline forcing anyone's hand.

## Why the $79,000-to-$81,000 Zone Matters So Much Right Now

Technical resistance levels aren't magic lines - they matter because enough market participants have decided they matter, which becomes a self-fulfilling pattern as automated trading systems and momentum traders cluster their orders around the same price points. In Bitcoin's case, the $80,000-to-$83,000 band carries extra significance because it overlaps with the 50-week moving average near $81,000 and the May 2026 swing high closer to $82,800 - two separate technical ceilings stacked almost on top of each other. Clearing that zone convincingly would remove two sources of overhead selling pressure at once; failing to clear it, as happened Friday, tends to trigger exactly the kind of pullback Bitcoin just went through.

The macro backdrop layered on top of that technical picture is what makes this particular test worth watching closely. The Federal Reserve's September policy meeting is scheduled for September 16, and as of this weekend, CME FedWatch pricing puts the odds of a 25-basis-point hike in the high-50% range - a coin-flip outcome that was sitting closer to 30% just two weeks earlier, before Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks. Because Bitcoin generates no yield or cash flow of its own, its valuation leans heavily on expectations about future liquidity conditions, which makes it unusually sensitive to shifts in rate-hike odds. A token trying to reclaim $80,000 while the market simultaneously prices in higher odds of tighter policy is fighting two forces at once - and Sunday's stall just below $79,000 is a fairly clean illustration of that tension playing out in real time.

There's also a regulatory undercurrent worth noting. Congress's long-running CLARITY Act, which would establish a clearer federal framework for digital-asset market structure, remains an open catalyst that traders are watching heading into September alongside the FOMC decision. Progress or setbacks on that front have moved crypto-linked stocks like Coinbase in the past, and with both the Fed meeting and potential legislative action landing in the same month, September is shaping up as a genuinely pivotal stretch for how this consolidation resolves.

Institutional demand has been the steadiest force underneath all of this volatility. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs pulled in more than $2.72 billion in net inflows during August alone, already surpassing April's previous monthly record, and that flow of buying has repeatedly cushioned pullbacks throughout the month even as short-term price action has whipsawed. Corporate treasury buyers have added to that steady bid too - asset manager Strive has continued adding to its Bitcoin holdings through multiple purchases in August, part of a broader pattern of public companies treating Bitcoin dips as buying opportunities rather than reasons to sell.

## What to Take Away From This

- **A range-bound market isn't a broken market.** Bitcoin has traded between roughly $75,000 and $81,000 for close to two weeks, testing both boundaries repeatedly. That kind of back-and-forth consolidation after a sharp run-up is a normal part of how markets digest a big move, not necessarily a sign the trend has reversed.
- **Watch where the moving averages sit, not just the daily headline.** Bitcoin holding above its 20-day, 50-day, and 200-day moving averages through a volatile week is a meaningfully different signal than a token that has broken below them - even when the day-to-day price swings look similarly dramatic in both cases.
- **Clustered resistance levels take more than one attempt to clear.** When multiple technical ceilings sit close together, as the 50-week moving average and the May swing high do here, expect several tries and possibly several pullbacks before a level like $81,000 gives way for good, if it does at all.
- **Institutional flows and short-term price action can tell different stories.** Even as Bitcoin whipsawed within a $4,000 range over three days, spot ETF inflows for the month kept setting records - a reminder that steady, longer-horizon demand often sits underneath much noisier short-term trading.

## FAQ

### Is Bitcoin's rebound to $78,960 a sign the Warsh-driven selloff is over?
It's too early to say definitively. Sunday's move recovers only part of Saturday's decline, and Bitcoin remains below both Friday's high and the key resistance band between $80,000 and $83,000. A more convincing signal would be a decisive close above $81,000 on strong volume, rather than a single day's bounce inside an already-established trading range.

### Why does a single Fed speech move Bitcoin's price so much?
Bitcoin has no cash flow, dividend, or yield of its own, so a large share of its valuation reflects expectations about future liquidity and interest rates rather than any measurable cash-generating fundamentals. That makes it one of the more rate-sensitive assets that trades, and it's amplified further by how much leverage typically sits in crypto derivatives markets compared with traditional equities.

### What should investors watch next for Bitcoin in September?
Two dates matter most: the Federal Reserve's September 16 policy decision, where odds of a hike have swung from roughly 30% to the high-50% range in under two weeks, and any progress on the CLARITY Act's digital-asset market-structure framework in Congress. Either could move Bitcoin sharply in either direction, and how the token behaves heading into both events should offer an early read on whether this consolidation resolves higher or lower.

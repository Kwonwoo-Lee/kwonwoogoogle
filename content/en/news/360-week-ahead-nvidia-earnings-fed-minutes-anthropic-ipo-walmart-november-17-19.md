## What Happened

Wall Street heads into the week of November 16 carrying an unusually dense stack of catalysts into just three trading sessions. Tuesday, November 17 alone brings three separate market-moving events: the October retail sales report at 8:30 a.m. ET, Home Depot's fiscal third-quarter earnings around 9 a.m. ET, and - the main event - Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter results after the closing bell. Wednesday, November 18 adds Target's earnings before the open, the Federal Reserve's minutes from its October 27-28 meeting at 2 p.m. ET, and the expected final pricing of Anthropic's initial public offering. Thursday, November 19 closes out the stretch with Walmart's earnings before market open and Anthropic shares beginning to trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT." Lowe's and TJX are also on the retail earnings calendar this week, according to multiple earnings-calendar trackers.

Nvidia's report carries the heaviest weight by far, and not just because of the numbers. It's the company's first full investor call since CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12 amid a board investigation into his personal conduct, with CFO Colette Kress stepping in as interim chief executive. Options traders were pricing in roughly a 9.6% potential swing for the stock around the print as of Thursday's close - the largest expected earnings-day move since 2024. Analyst consensus sits near $2.47-$2.49 in adjusted earnings per share on roughly $109 billion in revenue, which is at or above the top end of the $108 billion (plus or minus 2%) revenue guidance Nvidia issued back in August. The stock has fallen for six straight weeks, closing at $162.85 on Friday, November 13 after HSBC cut its price target from $375 to $170, pushing Nvidia's market capitalization below $4 trillion for the first time since July 2025.

Walmart's report is the week's other heavyweight. Analysts are modeling earnings per share of roughly $0.64 on revenue near $186.5 billion, representing year-over-year growth of about 3.2% and 3.9% respectively - a read on whether the resilient U.S. consumer that has powered retail results all year is still holding up heading into the holiday shopping season. Target and Home Depot, both of which have already shown diverging fortunes between everyday-consumables retail and big-ticket, rate-sensitive home improvement spending in earlier quarters this year, report in between.

## Why This Particular Week Matters More Than a Typical One

Most weeks on Wall Street have one or two scheduled catalysts that traders mark on a calendar well in advance. This week has at least five, and several of them interact with each other in ways that could amplify volatility rather than simply add it up. Start with the Fed minutes. They cover the October 27-28 meeting, which means they reflect the committee's thinking from before the hot October Consumer Price Index report that hit on November 10 and pushed futures-market pricing of a December rate hike toward roughly 90%. Reading minutes that predate the data everyone has since reacted to is an odd position for the market to be in: investors already know more about the inflation backdrop than the Fed officials did when they spoke those words, so the minutes will likely be parsed less for their original content and more for which officials were already leaning hawkish before the CPI print confirmed their instincts. That matters directly for how growth stocks like Nvidia get valued, since higher rate expectations raise the discount rate applied to future earnings.

Then there's the sequencing risk around Nvidia specifically. A governance crisis and an earnings report are two entirely different categories of risk, and this week forces the market to price both at once. A normal Nvidia earnings call draws questions about GPU demand, data-center capital spending from hyperscalers, and gross margin trends - all business-fundamentals questions with reasonably predictable ranges of outcomes. This call adds a second, far less quantifiable layer: whether Colette Kress's answers, tone, and command of the room during her first appearance as interim CEO reassure investors that the company's customer relationships and supply agreements have stayed intact through a month of leadership chaos, or whether uncertainty about a permanent CEO timeline continues to weigh on the stock regardless of what the income statement shows.

The Anthropic IPO adds a third dimension that's easy to overlook amid the Nvidia and Fed headlines, but it's arguably just as structurally important. If pricing on November 18 and the Nasdaq debut on November 19 go smoothly, it would mark the first successful public listing by a frontier AI lab - a test case that OpenAI and other AI companies eyeing their own future listings will be watching closely. It also closes a loop that started with Anthropic's amended S-1 filing on November 10: seven of its own suppliers and customers, including Nvidia and Google at $10 billion each, are simultaneously buying $26 billion of the company's non-voting shares in a side private placement. How investors receive that circular-financing structure in the open market - a strong debut versus a weak one - will say something about how much appetite is actually left for AI-adjacent risk after a month that has included a near-$1 trillion AI-stock drawdown scare and Nvidia's own leadership turmoil.

Finally, the retail earnings cluster offers a cleaner read on the economy underneath all the AI-sector noise. Walmart, Target, Home Depot, and Lowe's collectively cover a meaningful cross-section of how American households are spending heading into the holidays, from everyday groceries to discretionary home projects. Because this round of reports lands the same week as the delayed-reaction Fed minutes and a hot CPI print already in the rearview mirror, any softness in consumer spending numbers would add to the case for rate cuts eventually returning to the conversation - even as futures markets are currently leaning the opposite direction, toward a hike.

## What to Take Away From This

- **When multiple catalysts stack in the same week, each one can amplify the others rather than simply adding up independently.** A hawkish Fed minutes reading landing the same week as a shaky Nvidia earnings report, for instance, could compound into a larger move than either event would produce alone.
- **Minutes that predate the latest data point are a lagging indicator dressed up as a forward-looking one.** The October Fed minutes reflect thinking from before the hot October CPI report; reading them as a live signal of current Fed intentions, rather than a historical snapshot investors have already moved past, is a common mistake.
- **A first earnings call under new leadership carries two separate risk categories that deserve separate analysis.** Business fundamentals (revenue, margins, guidance) and governance confidence (does interim leadership command the room) can diverge, and a strong quarter doesn't automatically neutralize doubts about leadership stability.
- **A landmark IPO's reception tells you about sector-wide risk appetite, not just one company's valuation.** How Anthropic's Nasdaq debut trades will be read widely as a signal of how much enthusiasm investors still have left for AI-adjacent bets after a volatile month, which can spill over into sentiment on already-public AI infrastructure names.
- **Retail earnings remain one of the most direct windows into real consumer behavior, independent of whatever is dominating tech headlines.** Comparable-sales trends and forward guidance from Walmart, Target, Home Depot, and Lowe's this week are worth tracking on their own merits, separate from the AI-sector drama playing out simultaneously.

## FAQ

### What is the single most important event of the week for the overall stock market?
Nvidia's earnings report on Tuesday, November 17 after the close is the consensus pick, given the stock's heavy index weighting, the size of the options market's implied move (around 9.6%), and the fact that it's the company's first call under interim CEO Colette Kress. That said, the Fed minutes the next day and the Anthropic IPO debut on Thursday both carry real potential to move sentiment independently.

### Why do the October Fed minutes matter if the market already has November's inflation data?
Because the minutes reveal how individual Fed officials were thinking and voting before the hot October CPI report came out on November 10. Investors use that detail to gauge which officials were already leaning toward a December rate hike on their own reasoning, versus which ones might have been swayed primarily by the inflation surprise - useful context for forecasting how the committee might vote at the December 9-10 meeting.

### Is Anthropic's IPO connected to Nvidia's stock performance?
Indirectly, yes. Nvidia has committed $10 billion to buy Anthropic shares in a private placement tied to the IPO, meaning Nvidia's balance sheet now carries direct exposure to how Anthropic performs as a public company. A strong Anthropic debut would be a mild positive data point for Nvidia's own investment thesis in AI infrastructure spending, though the sums involved are small relative to Nvidia's own market capitalization.

### Could a weak retail earnings season change the Fed's rate path?
It's possible but not guaranteed on this timeline. Futures markets are currently leaning toward a December rate hike based largely on the hot October CPI report, and a single week of retail earnings is unlikely to reverse that on its own. A broader pattern of consumer weakness across several data points, rather than one week's results, would be needed to meaningfully shift rate expectations.
